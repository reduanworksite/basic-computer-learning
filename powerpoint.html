Lesson 5 • Microsoft PowerPoint

Microsoft PowerPoint Lessons

PowerPoint is for presentations — one idea per slide, shown one after another. Keep slides short, clear and visual.

What is a slide?

A presentation is a set of slides. Each slide is one page. When you present, you show the slides one by one and talk about each one.

5 step-by-step lessons

Lesson 1 — Create your first presentation

  1. Open PowerPoint and click Blank Presentation.
  2. Slide 1 appears with a title box. Click it and type your title.
  3. Click the subtitle box and type your name.
  4. Save with Ctrl + S — name it my-first-presentation.

Lesson 2 — Add slides and choose a layout

  1. Click Home → New Slide (or Ctrl + M).
  2. Choose a layout — Title and Content is the best one to start with.
  3. Type a short heading on each slide, then 3–4 short bullet points below it.
  4. Rule: one idea per slide. If a slide is full of text, split it into two slides.

Lesson 3 — Design: make slides look good

  1. Click the Design tab and pick a theme — one clean theme for the whole presentation.
  2. Use a large font for headings (28 or more) and a smaller one for points (20–24).
  3. Use dark text on a light background — easy to read from far away.
  4. Do not use more than 2 fonts and 2–3 colours in one presentation.

Lesson 4 — Add pictures and transitions

  1. Click Insert → Pictures to add a photo that fits your topic.
  2. Click the Transitions tab, pick one simple transition — e.g. Fade.
  3. Use the same transition for every slide. Too many effects look messy.
  4. Click a picture to resize it — drag a corner, not a side, so it does not stretch.

Lesson 5 — Present with confidence

  1. Press F5 to start the slide show from the beginning.
  2. Click or press → (right arrow) for the next slide. Press ← to go back.
  3. Press Esc to stop presenting.
  4. Speak slowly, look at your audience, and do not read the slides word for word — the slides are hints, you are the speaker.

Good slides vs bad slides

Good ✓Bad ✗
Short heading + 3–4 bullet pointsA whole paragraph on one slide
One clear picture per slideMany pictures fighting for space
Large, readable textTiny text nobody can read
One theme, one transitionA different effect on every slide
Presenting tip: Practise your presentation out loud at least twice before the real day. Time yourself — a good short presentation is 5–10 minutes.
