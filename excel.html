Lesson 4 • Microsoft Excel

Microsoft Excel Lessons

Excel is for numbers — marks, expenses, lists and accounts. Type numbers into cells, and let formulas do the maths for you.

Cells, rows and columns

An Excel file is a workbook. Each workbook has worksheets (the tabs at the bottom). A worksheet is a grid:

  • Column: a vertical line of cells, named by a letter — A, B, C…
  • Row: a horizontal line of cells, named by a number — 1, 2, 3…
  • Cell: one box where a row and a column meet. Its name is its column + row — e.g. B3.

5 step-by-step lessons

Lesson 1 — Open Excel and type your first numbers

  1. Open Excel and click Blank workbook.
  2. Click cell A1 and type Item. Press Tab to move to B1.
  3. In B1 type Price. Press Enter to move down.
  4. Fill in 3 items and their prices below.
  5. Save with Ctrl + S.

Lesson 2 — Your first formula: SUM

  1. Click the empty cell below your prices.
  2. Type =SUM(B2:B4) — change B2:B4 to match your own rows.
  3. Press Enter. Excel adds the numbers for you.
  4. Change one price — the total updates by itself. That is the power of formulas.
  5. Every formula starts with =.

Lesson 3 — AVERAGE and COUNT

  1. =AVERAGE(B2:B4) finds the average (total ÷ how many).
  2. =COUNT(B2:B4) counts how many cells have numbers.
  3. =MAX(B2:B4) finds the biggest number; =MIN(B2:B4) the smallest.
  4. Try them in the mini spreadsheet above first, then in real Excel.

Lesson 4 — Format numbers and cells

  1. Select the price cells.
  2. On the Home tab, make the heading row bold.
  3. Use the number format to show prices with 2 decimal places, e.g. 150.00.
  4. Change a column width by dragging the line between two column letters.
  5. Save with Ctrl + S.

Lesson 5 — Make a simple chart

  1. Select your items and prices (including the headings).
  2. Click the Insert tab, then Column Chart.
  3. Excel draws a chart from your numbers — taller bar, bigger number.
  4. Click the chart title and type a clear title, e.g. My Expenses.
  5. Change a price — the chart changes too.

Excel formulas — quick reference

FormulaWhat it doesExample
=SUM(A1:A5)Adds the numbers10 + 20 + 30 = 60
=AVERAGE(A1:A5)Finds the averageAverage of 10, 20, 30 = 20
=COUNT(A1:A5)Counts cells with numbers5 numbers → 5
=MAX(A1:A5)Biggest numberMax of 10, 20, 30 = 30
=MIN(A1:A5)Smallest numberMin of 10, 20, 30 = 10
=A1+B1Adds two cellsSimple maths, cell by cell
